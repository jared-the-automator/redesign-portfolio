Commissioning a hand-carved duck

The questions Rick hears most. If yours is not here, ask him.

How does a commission work?

Tell Rick the species, hen or drake, the pose, and who the bird is for. A 50% deposit reserves your place, and Rick sends an estimated completion date.

While he works, Rick emails progress photos every couple of days. He carves one or two birds a month. Start a commission.

How much does a hand-carved duck cost?

Most full-detail birds cost $1,500 to $2,500, depending on the size, the species and the amount of detail. Birds in the Duck Shop are often less: the current pieces are $695 to $795.

How long is the wait?

Rick is often booked about six months out. For a birthday or holiday, order early or give a commission gift certificate.

What wood do you use?

Most birds are tupelo or basswood, soft woods with almost no knots or grain. Some shop pieces are sugar pine, pine or cedar.

How long does one bird take?

Usually 50 to 80 hours, depending on the size and complexity of the bird. A mallard drake or wood duck takes longer, because of the vermiculation on the sides.

Do you ship?

Yes. Every bird ships insured, and Rick's clients live everywhere from Arizona to Maine.

What comes with a gift certificate?

The certificate names the bird and its estimated arrival date. Rick also sends a Wooden Bird coffee mug as a stocking stuffer.

Are these working decoys?

No. Rick's birds are decorative, made for a shelf or mantel. They are still built true to life: competition birds must float in a lifelike manner, and each one gets a float test on Rick's pond before a show.

Ready to own a hand-carved bird?

Rick carves one or two birds a month, and most are commissions. Tell him the species and the occasion, and he will send an estimated completion date.
