From block to bird

Shaped by hand until it breathes

Rick spends 50 to 80 hours on a bird, depending on its size and detail. Commission clients follow every step.

  1. Rick studies thousands of reference photos, pencils the pattern on tupelo or basswood, and cuts it out on the band saw.

  2. A heavy grinder takes the block to a body. Then smaller bits carve the feather groups, the bill and the head.

  3. Every feather and every barb is raised with dental-type bits and a wood burner. Glass eyes go in last.

  4. Five to seven thin layers of acrylic, then hundreds of fine lines with a small brush. Rick calls it putting on the makeup.

  5. Competition birds must float in a lifelike manner, so each one gets a float test on the pond before a show.

Commission clients see it happen. Rick emails photos every couple of days, from the first cut to the last brushstroke. One client compared it to watching his garden grow.
