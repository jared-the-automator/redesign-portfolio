Hand-Carved Mallard Decoys

Rick has carved more mallards than any other duck, from resting drakes to hens with a line of ducklings. Each one starts as a block of tupelo and is painted in many thin layers of acrylic, so the feathers look soft enough to touch.

What makes Rick's mallards different

  • Carved by hand from kiln-dried tupelo, basswood or sugar pine
  • Every feather textured and wood-burned, then painted in 5 to 7 thin layers
  • Drakes, hens, pairs, and hens with ducklings
  • One of a kind: no two birds are the same
  • Signed by Rick, with a letter about how your bird was made

Field notes

Mallard

The drake has a green head, yellow bill, white neck ring and reddish chest, while the hen is mottled brown with black smudges on an orange bill. Mallards live almost anywhere with fresh water, from marshes and rivers to city parks. Pairs form in fall and winter, and the hen alone sits on the eggs for 23 to 30 days.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.
