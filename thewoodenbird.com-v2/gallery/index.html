Field notes

Mallard

The drake has a green head, yellow bill, white neck ring and reddish chest, while the hen is mottled brown with black smudges on an orange bill. Mallards live almost anywhere with fresh water, from marshes and rivers to city parks. Pairs form in fall and winter, and the hen alone sits on the eggs for 23 to 30 days.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.

Field notes

Hens and their broods

A Mallard hen builds her nest on the ground, lined with down, and sits on the eggs by herself for 23 to 30 days. Soon after the ducklings hatch, she leads them to water. They find their own food while she watches over them. The young take their first flights at about 52 to 60 days old.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.

Field notes

Wood Duck

Wood Ducks favor wooded ponds, swamps and slow rivers, and they nest in tree cavities up to 65 feet above the ground. The morning after hatching, the ducklings climb to the cavity opening and jump, clinging with sharp claws and bracing with their tails. The male is colorful for most of the year, and the female shows a white eye patch.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.

Field notes

Northern Pintail

The Northern Pintail is slim and long-necked, and the drake has a white stripe running up his neck onto a brown head, with a long pointed tail. Hens are mottled buff-brown. They summer on prairies, farmland and tundra. Pintail nests sit on dry ground among short plants, and the hen leads her ducklings away within hours of hatching.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.

Field notes

Common Loon

In summer the Common Loon has a black head, a black-and-white striped collar and a boldly checkered back. It lives on large northern lakes and dives to catch fish. Its loud, wailing call carries across the water, often at night. The chicks leave the nest within a day or two of hatching and sometimes ride on a parent's back.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.

Field notes

Blue-winged Teal

The Blue-winged Teal is a small duck with a pale blue wing patch that is usually hidden on the water and shows in flight. Breeding drakes have a white crescent on a gray face. It feeds in shallow marshes and ponds and migrates late in spring and early in fall. The Green-winged Teal, North America's smallest dabbling duck, has a chestnut head with a green ear patch.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.

Field notes

Canvasback

The Canvasback is a large diving duck, and its best field mark is the long, sloping profile of its head and bill. The drake has a chestnut head, black chest and whitish back, and the hen is pale tan with a grayish body. It dives for food in water a few feet deep. The hen builds a bulky nest in dense marsh plants.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.

Field notes

Mergansers

The Hooded Merganser is a crow-sized duck whose drake has a white crest with a black border that can be raised or flattened. It lives on wooded ponds and rivers and dives for small fish. It nests in tree cavities and in nest boxes. Within 24 hours of hatching, the ducklings climb to the opening and jump to the ground.

The Red-breasted Merganser has gray sides and a dark red breast, and it is not as white as the Common Merganser. Its shaggy crest is hard to see from far away, but its white collar stands out. In winter it is found mostly on coastal bays and estuaries.

Field notes adapted from the Audubon Field Guide.
